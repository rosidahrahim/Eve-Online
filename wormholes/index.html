    Statics by class

    Every home system carries one static, or two in C2 and C4. When a static collapses, a new one spawns straight away to the same class of space.

    Spot it in space

    What a hole looks like tells you where it goes and how big a ship fits, before you open Show Info. Treat the look as a first guess and confirm in Show Info. Looking at the hole turns the skybox, which can show more detail.

    Quick rules

    White aura
    Cannot lead to C1. A C1 connection is never larger than medium, apart from frigate holes.
    Orange aura
    A C4 connection is never very large, so an orange aura or very large capacity points to C5.
    Hole age
    With 24 to 4 hours left the aurora wiggles more. Under 4 hours it fades and loses defined shapes.
    Mass shrink
    The hole visibly shrinks when it drops to 50% and again at 10% mass.

    Show Info states the real numbers: lifetime of more than a day, under 4 hours, under 1 hour or expired, and mass of more than 50%, under 50% or under 10%. The wiki says claims that the effect intensifies near closing are anecdotal.

    Rolling guide

    Rolling is collapsing a hole on purpose by sending a heavy ship through it until its mass limit runs out. You roll to cut off a threat, to reset a static, or to find a new connection. The method below uses a battleship, with the Praxis as the worked example.

    How the mass works

    Hot and cold
    Hot means the propulsion module is on during the jump, cold means off. Turning it on adds mass.
    Same size, same mass
    An afterburner and a microwarpdrive of the same size add the same mass.
    Higgs anchor
    The large Higgs Anchor rig is the key part of a rolling fit. The All-Out guide gives no numeric effect for it.
    Hole total
    Each hole is born with 90% to 110% of its listed total mass, so you never know the exact limit.
    Every jump counts
    Mass is spent in both directions. A round trip costs your cold mass plus your hot mass, or hot twice.

    Reference masses from the All-Out guide: Megathron hot with Higgs 300M kg, cold with Higgs 200M, hot unrigged 150M, cold unrigged 100M. Thorax hot with Higgs 125M, cold 25M. The unrigged pair shows the prop module adds about 50M kg, and the rigged pair shows it adding about 100M.

    The Praxis hull is light next to a Megathron, so it needs about 113M kg of fitted mass (armor plates, rig, modules) to reach a 200M cold weight. The ship info window shows the fitted mass, so fit it, then toggle the prop module and read the cold and hot numbers yourself. A forum thread on Praxis rollers gives its own cold and hot figures, but another poster in the same thread says those numbers are wrong, so measure your own fit. Keep the hot weight under the hole's per-jump limit. If it comes out over, take plates offline rather than guessing.

    The wiki's own rolling guides quote the large-hole jump limit as both 300M and 375M kg, and its attributes table says 375M. Read Show Info on the hole you are rolling and keep your hot mass under that number.

    Step by step

    1. Check the hole. Show Info gives the destination, the lifetime state, and the mass state (more than 50%, under 50%, or under 10% left). Find its total mass in the chart above.
    2. Prepare. Fit the roller with a scanner and probes so you can find your way home. Bookmark the hole on both sides. Tell a second pilot where you are.
    3. Start with hot and cold passes. On a fresh hole, All-Out alternates cold and hot passes from inside the hole, watching for the mass state to change. Its safe pattern for a 2,000M hole is cold out, hot in, repeated, with the last pass chosen from what Show Info reports.
    4. Wait out polarization. After each jump you are polarized for 5 minutes, so each pass takes time. Use that time to check Show Info.
    5. Watch for reduced. When Show Info says under 50% left, the hole has taken about half its total mass. This narrows the unknown 10% spread.
    6. Watch for critical. Critical means under 10% left. The most mass a critical hole can still hold is 11% of its listed total. If your hot mass is above that, one hot pass finishes it.
    7. Finish on the right side. If the hole turns critical while you are outside, a hot pass back in rolls it. If you are inside, use a cheap threader ship with a cheap clone: go cold out and hot in until the hole closes or rolls you out.
    8. If it rolls early. If the hole closes before critical, you may be stranded on the far side. Probe your way home, and keep the roller insured.

    All-Out reports its method fails about 5% of the time from random mass variation, and most other failures are human error. Once several ships of unknown mass have jumped, the sums stop being reliable.

    Reading the numbers

    K162
    Exit side of any hole. Jump through to read the real type.
    Total mass
    Every ship that jumps adds its mass until the hole collapses.
    Jump mass
    Heaviest single ship allowed through. Heavier ships cannot jump.
    Regen
    Mass a few holes win back each day; most never do.
    Wandering C4
    C4 only reaches K-space by wandering holes: S047 to highsec, N290 to lowsec, K329 to nullsec.
    Polarization
    After a jump you stay polarized for 5 minutes. Wait it out before the next pass.
